Her Voice, Her Power

I never thought I'd recover after the trauma. Every day felt like a blur. I never thought I’d be the woman who stayed. I was 24, fresh out of university, and completely in love with a man I thought I knew. The first time he slapped me, I was too shocked to cry. I remember standing there, paralyzed, while he begged me not to leave. He said he was sorry. That it was the stress. That I had made him angry. I believed him. Because I loved him. But love turned into fear. He controlled what I wore, who I spoke to, where I went. If I didn’t answer his calls fast enough, I’d be punished. At first, it was yelling. Then slaps. Then punches. I started wearing makeup just to cover the bruises, smiling through pain so no one would ask questions. One night, he accused me of cheating, locked the door, and took my phone. I was trapped for two days without food. It was in that moment—hungry, cold, and alone—that I realized: if I stayed, I would die. I had hidden a tiny phone in my sock drawer that he didn’t know about. I texted my friend, and she came with the police. That was the last time I saw him. I moved in with a relative in another town, enrolled in therapy, and slowly began to rebuild my life. It wasn’t easy. The trauma didn’t disappear overnight. But I am healing. Today, I work with a local women's organization, helping survivors escape and recover. I tell my story not for pity—but to remind others that survival is possible. Choosing life was the hardest decision I ever made. But it saved me. But speaking up connected me to others who reminded me of my strength. Healing doesn't mean forgetting — it means reclaiming power. If you're reading this, I hope you know you're stronger than your story.
