Her Voice, Her Power

I was scared to speak. Scared of what people would say. But silence weighed more heavily than judgment ever could.Growing up, I was taught to stay silent. “Good girls don’t talk back,” they’d say. In my rural community, it’s common for girls to be married off by the time they’re 17. I thought it was normal—until it almost became my reality. When I turned 16, my uncle—who was my guardian after my parents passed—told me I would marry a 45-year-old man from a neighboring county. I refused. I wanted to finish school. I had dreams of becoming a lawyer. He didn’t care. When I spoke out, he called me disrespectful. He locked me in a room for three days without food, threatening to “teach me how girls behave.” I was terrified. But I remembered something my high school teacher once said: “Silence is consent.” With the little strength I had, I broke a window and ran—barefoot—until I reached a nearby chief's camp. They connected me to a rescue center that took me in. At the shelter, I found my voice again. I went back to school. I met girls who had faced even worse, and together, we shared our stories. I spoke at a local community meeting, publicly denouncing child marriage. My voice trembled, but it was heard. Today, I live with a loving host family and mentor younger girls in my village about their rights, safety, and education. I am no longer a victim—I am a voice. And I will never be silenced again. Writing my truth gave me peace. My scars are real, but so is my joy. To every survivor your story is your own, and it matters deeply.
