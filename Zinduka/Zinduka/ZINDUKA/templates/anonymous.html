Her Voice, Her Power

For years I struggled with depression in silence. No one could see it on my face. It was heavy... unbearable. But writing this is proof: I made it through.

Sharing my story was the first step to healing. He was perfect in public—charming, funny, respectful. Everyone loved him. So when I told people he was hurting me, no one believed it. “Wanjiku, he would never!” But behind closed doors, he was controlling and violent. He isolated me from my family, took control of my finances, and humiliated me daily. The physical abuse started when I was pregnant with our first child. I stayed because I believed that a child needed both parents. I stayed because I was ashamed. The abuse escalated. I would wear long sleeves in the heat just to hide the bruises. I had to ask permission before leaving the house. I felt like a prisoner in my own home. Then one night, he pushed our daughter during a rage. That was it. I looked into her scared eyes and knew I had to leave—not just for me, but for her. I packed what I could and fled to a women’s refuge. We had nothing but each other. At the shelter, I learned about trauma, healing, and self-worth. I started sewing to earn money. One dress turned into ten, then a small business. Now I run a community tailoring shop where I train other survivors to earn a living. Each stitch is a step toward freedom. My past may be broken, but my future is bold. I am not just surviving—I am thriving. And I will keep fighting for women like me to have a second chance at life. I want you to know — you are not alone. Speak, write, cry, shout — do what you must to reclaim your light.
